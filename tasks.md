## Household aisles

Goal: moving an ingredient to another aisle changes it for everyone in the household, not just the person who did it. Today an aisle choice is a per-user override (`user_category_overrides`, spec 4.3.2) and custom aisles belong to one user (`categories.owner_user_id`). Both become household data: an override is keyed by `(household_id, ingredient_name)`, and a custom aisle belongs to a household, so every member has it. Each person keeps their own aisle **order** (`user_category_order` stays per user). Someone in two households has a separate set of overrides and custom aisles in each, and a list or recipe uses its own household's. "Reset to default" resets it for the whole household. When two members change the same ingredient, the later change wins (field-level LWW through `apply_mutations`, like other household data). No existing aisle data is kept: per-user overrides and custom aisles are dropped, on the server and on the device, along with any order rows that point at custom aisles. Don't migrate or move any of it. Spec sections to rewrite as you go: 2 (req 6's row says "Per-user recategorization"), 3 (the Override glossary entry), 4.3.2, 4.7's "Per-user settings stay per user" paragraph, 5 (data model, "Effective category", row-level security), 7 (sync: per-user vs household tables, realtime channels), 9's acceptance test 7, and Q4 in the open questions. Each task updates the spec sections for what it changes. Don't push to or deploy hosted Supabase; that's done by hand.

## Email invites

Goal: inviting someone to a household starts with their email, then the inviter picks how to tell them (Messages, Mail, Copy link, More…). The invite is attached to the email on the server, so it shows as pending on the household screen and appears in the invitee's app when they sign in with that address, even if they never tap the link (that also covers someone who installs the app after getting the text). The link still works for whoever opens it first, as now: the email decides who sees the invite in the app, but doesn't lock the link (Google-only sign-in means people often have more than one account). The app sends no email itself; the inviter sends it through Messages or their own Mail. A small "Share a link instead" option keeps today's link-only invite for when the inviter doesn't know the address. Links keep the `perfectbite://` scheme for now; `https` links need a hosted domain (separate follow-up). Designs: https://claude.ai/artifact/P4t5MvEdV3YmeKCnZmoXfi (copy in `docs/design/invites.html`; screens S1–S4 for sending, R1–R3 for receiving; R1's rich preview and R2a's web page wait for the domain). Each task updates the spec sections for what it changes (4.7 Invites, 5's schema and `household_invites` RLS paragraph, M7 in 10) and adds any new component pattern to `DESIGN.md`. Don't push to or deploy hosted Supabase; that's done by hand.
