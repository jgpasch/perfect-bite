## Household activity

Goal: you can tell what the rest of the household did to a list. When you open a list and someone else added items since you last looked, a card says so ("Alex added 4 items") and those rows are marked **NEW** until you leave (board 1 A). While you're looking at a list and someone else adds to it, the new row slides in tagged with their name, and the header says "Alex is adding" while they are (board 2 D and E, and the prototype). Mockups: https://claude.ai/artifact/7YVHMBerTkBufhm8CcVv8C. Boards 1 B and C and board 2 F aren't built. Every string is in `en.ts` and `es.ts`. The tasks run in order: the first one puts members' names on the device, and the rest need them.

## Step ingredients in cook mode

Goal: in cook mode you stop scrolling between the ingredient checklist and the steps. An AI call works out which ingredients each step uses. The match is saved on the recipe and synced, so cook mode stays fast and works offline. Each step then lists its own ingredients (amounts and checks) right under it (board 3 G and I). A recipe with no match yet opens cook mode as it does today (board 4 L). The match shows only in cook mode: the recipe screen doesn't change, and there's no way to edit a match by hand. Mockups: https://claude.ai/artifact/7YVHMBerTkBufhm8CcVv8C. Follow `docs/llm-calls.md` "Adding a new LLM task". Every string is in `en.ts` and `es.ts`. The tasks run in order. The first task is an eval: stop after it if the results aren't good enough.

- [x] The eval first: can a model match ingredients to steps? (spec 6; `docs/spikes.md`). Add an AI task `match-recipe-steps` in `_shared/ai/tasks.ts`, with a versioned prompt (`prompts/match-recipe-steps.v1.ts`) and `AI_MODEL_STEPS` (default the recipe model; optional `_FALLBACK`). Input: the steps (numbered) and the ingredients (numbered: name, quantity, unit, note). Output (Zod): for each step, a list of `{ ingredient: <index>, quantity: number | null }`, where `quantity` is that step's share in the ingredient's own unit when the recipe splits it ("juice of 1 lime" from 3 limes), and null for all of it or not stated. Validation drops unknown indexes and repeats within a step, nulls a share that isn't positive or is over the total, and turns a step count that doesn't match into `unavailable`. The prompt: the recipe is untrusted text; only ingredients the step actually uses; "the chicken" in step 5 means the cooked chicken from step 1, which is not a new use; a pantry staple named in no step stays unplaced. Add the eval at `supabase/functions/match-recipe-steps/eval/`: 15 or more recipes from the import eval's fixtures that have steps, with hand-labelled expected matches in `expected.json`. `yarn ai:eval:steps [--model …]` prints per-recipe and overall precision and recall of (step, ingredient) pairs, the share of ingredients placed nowhere, split-amount accuracy, and median and p95 latency and cost per recipe. Run it on the current recipe model and on at least one cheaper model. Also add `yarn ai:steps` for trying one recipe by hand, like `yarn ai:tags`, with an in-memory store and the real model unless `--model mock`. It accepts a recipe link (`yarn ai:steps https://…`: the import's own fetch and extraction get the steps and ingredients, with nothing cached or written), `--file page.html`, or `--step "…"` and `--ingredient "2 lb chicken thighs"` flags, repeated, for a recipe typed in. It prints each numbered step, then its matched ingredients indented under it with amounts and shares ("Limes · 1 of 3"), then "Not in a step" with the unplaced ones, then the model, the prompt version, the latency and the tokens. It also takes `--model`, `--raw` (the model's JSON as returned), `--json` and `--quiet`, as `ai:tags` does. Document both scripts in `docs/llm-calls.md` "Running a call yourself". Write the results and a go/no-go with the chosen model in `docs/spikes.md` §5 ("Step ingredients"). Suggested bar: precision ≥ 0.95 (a wrong ingredient on a step is worse than a missing one) and recall ≥ 0.85. No app or database changes in this task. Tests: the schema and validation (bad index, repeat, a share over the total, step count mismatch); the mock model, which matches by name words appearing in the step text; the eval scorer on a tiny fixture. If the bar isn't met, mark this done, write up why in the spikes doc, and stop: tell the user in progress.md that the rest of the section is on hold.
