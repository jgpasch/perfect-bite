Inviting someone to a household

Ask for the person's email first, then let the inviter choose how to tell them. The email is what makes the invite reach the right person, even someone who doesn't have the app yet. The link is just one way of telling them.

What happens today

  1. Household screen → Invite someone.
  2. The app calls create_invite and opens the iOS share sheet with "Join “Smith household” on The Perfect Bite: perfectbite://invite/…".
  3. Whoever taps the link sees the join screen, if they have the app and are signed in.

Where it breaks down

  • A perfectbite:// link does nothing for someone who hasn't installed the app. Messages may not even make it tappable.
  • If they're signed out, the link is dropped after sign-in (the spec lists this as a follow-up).
  • The inviter can't see who has been invited, and can't cancel or resend an invite. The server never knows who an invite was meant for.

How other apps handle it

Apps mostly fall into three patterns. The one you described is the third, and it's what most current apps do.

1 · Link only

WhatsApp group links, Splitwise's invite link. This is what you have now.

  • Nothing to build on the server, works in any chat app
  • Anyone holding the link can join
  • Nothing to show as "pending"
  • Needs working universal links plus deferred deep linking to cover new users

2 · Email, sent by the app

Slack, Notion, Google Docs, AnyList. You type an address and the app's server sends the email.

  • Invite is tied to one person
  • Shows as pending, and the invite is waiting when they sign up
  • Needs a mail provider, a sending domain, templates and deliverability work
  • An email from an unfamiliar sender is easy to ignore. A text from your partner isn't.
Recommended

3 · Email first, you choose how to send

Apple's sharing sheet in Notes, Reminders and Files (Messages / Mail / Copy link, plus who can join). Todoist and Figma work the same way.

  • Invite is tied to an email: it shows as pending and appears in the app when they sign in
  • The message comes from the inviter, through Messages or their own Mail
  • No mail provider needed. Server-sent email can be added later as one more option.
  • Adds one step before sharing

Decisions for you

1. Who can use an email invite's link?

Recommend: anyone who opens it, once (as now). The email decides who sees the invite in the app, but it doesn't lock the link.

Locking it to the email is stricter, but with Google-only sign-in people often have more than one account. "This invite is for sam.lee@gmail.com, you're signed in as sam@work.com" is a frustrating dead end in a two-person household app.

2. Should the app send the email itself?

Recommend: not in v1. The inviter sends it through Messages or Mail. Add a "Send email for me" option later, from an Edge Function using a provider such as Resend, once there's a domain.

Supabase's built-in mailer only sends auth emails, so this needs its own provider, sending domain and templates.

3. Is a hosted domain in scope now?

Recommend: yes, as its own task first. R1 and R2a both need it: an https link, an apple-app-site-association file and a one-page landing site. Without it, the email-plus-in-app path (R2b) still works, but the link only helps people who already have the app.

4. Keep a link-only option?

Recommend: yes, as the small link in S2. It covers "I'll just AirDrop it" and not knowing which Google address they use.

Suggested order

  1. Email invites without a domain: schema, RPCs, the S2/S3 sheet, the S4 pending list and the R2b card. These work as soon as they ship, because the in-app card doesn't need links.
  2. Hosted domain: https links, AASA, the landing page (R2a) and rich previews (R1).
  3. Later: "Send email for me", the contact picker and push notifications for invites.
